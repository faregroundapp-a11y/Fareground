Fareground turns the steps you already take into ground you own. Every 100 steps is a Walk Point. Spend Walk Points on a real 9-metre square of the world near you, and it pays you coins every hour from then on — whether the app is open or not.

The loop

Three things happen, in this order.

No energy bars, no waiting for a timer. The only input the game wants is the walk you were going to do anyway.

01

Your steps become Walk Points

Fareground reads the step count your phone already keeps — Health Connect on Android, motion history on iOS — plus the live sensor while you walk. Nothing to start, nothing to stop.

100 steps = 1 WP
02

Spend them on ground you're standing near

The map lights up the squares within reach. Pick one, claim it, watch the block erupt. You can only claim where you actually are — and each square on Earth has exactly one owner, ever.

40 m reach · ~9 m squares
03

It pays you, hour after hour

Every parcel turns out to be a mineral. Rocky ground trickles. A ruby pours. The coins accumulate while the app is closed, and you collect them whenever you open it.

3–25 coins a month, forever
What's under the ground

You don't know what you've claimed until you claim it.

Five minerals, one roll per parcel. A ruby is a one-in-a-hundred square that earns eight times what plain rock does — and the phone thumps harder the rarer the find.

The coins

Straight about the money.

Coins are counted as whole numbers in a ledger that never gets rewritten — every single coin you've earned has a row with a reason attached. The plan is for them to be worth something real, and here is exactly what that rate looks like.

Cashing out is not switched on yet. Fareground is in beta, no one has been paid, and the redemption side needs to be built and checked properly before it is. Anything you bank now is kept and keeps earning — but treat the dollar figures as the design, not a promise.
